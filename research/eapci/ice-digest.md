## 1. Catheter types

**Mechanical (rotational / radial)** (E20 p.2-3, 6; E11 p.1-2; Fig 1)
- Crystal spins at the tip; image plane is perpendicular to catheter axis, IVUS-like 360° cross-section, catheter in the centre.
- Example: Ultra ICE, 9 Fr, 110 cm, 9 MHz; transducer in a 1 cm sheath filled with sterile water.
- Steered only by exchanging precurved Convoy sheaths (15° to 140° curves); 55° sheath used for the basic protocol.
- Pros: cheaper, sharp near field, shares IVUS console. Cons: no colour/spectral Doppler, shallow depth, stiff shaft.
- Display convention: CT/MRI-like orientation, left-sided structures on image right.

**Phased array** (E20 p.3-6; E11 p.2; Fig 2-3)
- 64-element array; familiar wedge (sector) image like TTE/TOE; sector set at 90° to the tip, side-looking, longitudinal plane along the shaft.
- 5 to 10 MHz: low frequency for far-field depth, high for near-field resolution.
- Full modes: M-mode, PW, CW, colour Doppler (key for shunt closure checks).
- AcuNav: 8 Fr/110 cm or 10 Fr/90 cm; 4-way tip (anterior/posterior and left/right), deflection up to 160°. ViewFlex PLUS: 9 Fr, A/P only, up to 120°; ViewFlex Xtra adds L/R.
- One-hand steering: axial rotation + tip deflection.
- 3D/4D (E20 p.4-5): AcuNav V (10 Fr, 90°x24° volume), AcuNav Volume (12.5 Fr, 90 cm, 90°x50°), NuVera 4D (10 Fr, 90°x90°). Experimental: HockeyStick 9 Fr 7-12 MHz side-looking; MicroLinear 9 Fr 14 MHz forward-looking with ablation tip; cMUT with 5 Fr working lumen. SoundStar used with CARTO mapping.
- Chapter's procedural descriptions all assume AcuNav (E20 p.6).

**Access / depth in practice**
- Femoral vein (right or left) → IVC → mid RA, inserted in neutral (E20 p.7). Left femoral vein frees the right for devices (E20 p.27).
- Figure screen settings (Fig 4-17): 7.5-10 MHz; displayed depth 50-130 mm, typically 80-110 mm.
- Catheters are single-use, sterile packed; certified re-sterilisation exists only in some countries (E20 p.2, 5).

## 2. Standard views (phased array, AcuNav), in textbook order

Frame: catheter in mid RA from IVC, neutral = no deflection, tip facing anteriorly toward the tricuspid valve. Rotation is clockwise from neutral in about 15° steps; each step brings more **posterior** structures into the sector (E20 p.7). Apex of the sector (near field) is always the RA side; numbers in brackets are the textbook's rotation angles.

**Mechanical (Ultra ICE) basic protocol** (E20 p.6-7; E11 p.3)
- 55° sheath over wire; catheter into SVC, then pulled back through RA into IVC.
- Pull-back gives 4 transverse slices: (1) great vessels, (2) SVC-RA junction, (3) aortic valve level, (4) IVC-RA junction / cavotricuspid isthmus.
- 4-chamber: mid RA, sheath turned posterior and leftward to show IAS; more CW gives 5-chamber.

**Septal sweep for ASD sizing** (E20 p.21): from short-axis view, advance into SVC (superior septum) then withdraw to IVC (inferior septum).

**Views from other chambers (non-standard)**
- **RV, rightward flex** (E20 p.23-25; Fig 16): cross TV in neutral, flex right → oblique long axis of LA, MV (AML/PML), LV; IVS at top (near field). Same manoeuvre shows layering pericardial effusion along inferior LV wall (E20 p.30).
- **RV, leftward flex** (Fig 17): LV short axis at mitral level; TV in near field, IVS, AML/PML. Caution: ectopy, RV free-wall perforation.
- **RVOT** (Fig 15): advance into RVOT → membranous septum, Ao next to RVOT (perimembranous VSD).
- **Via ASD into LA** (E20 p.26): 90° anterior flex (beam posterior) = long axis LA/MV/LV/AV; 45° flex = short axis; 90° with beam to apex = 4-chamber; tip anterior = 5-chamber.
- **LA via transseptal sheath, tip at LUPV ostium**, or **RVOT/pulmonary artery**: better LAA imaging for LAAO (E20 p.19, 30).
- Also: arterial use for abdominal aortic stent grafts. Extra risks of non-standard positions: access bleeding, thromboembolism, perforation (E20 p.19).
- VSD 4-chamber: mid RA, CW 60-70°, tilt right (LA, LV, IVS). VSD short axis: CW ~100° aiming posterior, tip near TV looking up to LVOT (E20 p.23).

Not described in this chapter: a dedicated esophagus view or a dedicated descending-aorta view (DAo only appears in the far field of Fig 10); no CS view.

## 3. Procedural uses

**Transseptal puncture** (E20 p.26-28)
- Target: mid septum; on lateral fluoroscopy posterior/inferior to AV (pigtail marker). Bulging septum, aneurysm, kyphosis, dilated root make fluoroscopy alone hard.
- ICE from left femoral vein; IAS long-axis view, then CW into septal short-axis view = monitoring view (BMV, VT ablation, LAAO).
- For AF/PVI choose a more posterior plane showing left PVs.
- Avoid an anterior plane containing aorta; superiorly aimed needle risks aortic puncture.
- Advance Brockenbrough needle until tenting; do not cross until entry is centred mid-septum.
- TOE preferred for edge-to-edge mitral clip: puncture height above mitral leaflets needs 2D/3D TOE; GA already used.

**ASD / PFO closure** (E20 p.19-22; Fig 14)
- Only secundum ASD suitable. PFO in ~20% of people.
- ICE tasks: count fenestrations, exclude anomalous PVs, measure rims, balloon sizing, colour flow; agitated saline for PFO.
- Rims in 6 sectors (superior, anterosuperior, anteroinferior, inferior, posteroinferior, posterosuperior); ≥5 mm each = adequate.
- Sequence: home (CW 15°), lock posterior flex, CW 90-100° to IAS long axis, CW to septal short axis (working view for wires and device), revisit 60-70° LA view if needed, final colour/saline in short axis.
- Fluoro-only series: complete closure 88-91% at 6 months; residual shunt minimal 6-7%, moderate 2-3%, large 1-2%.

**VSD closure** (E20 p.23-26): home view, short axis at ~100°, 4/5-chamber for muscular VSD, RV and RVOT views. Perimembranous VSD phase I: 3/32 (8.6%) serious events (AV block, TV injury, perihepatic bleed).

**Balloon mitral valvuloplasty** (E20 p.27-30; Fig 8, 18): cross septum, switch to MV/LV view (70-80°), guide Inoue balloon; PHT mitral area, CW/colour for MR; check iatrogenic ASD in septal short axis.

**LAA closure** (E20 p.30): TOE remains gold standard. ICE alternative; weakness is far-field LAA from RA. Fixes: ICE into LA via the same transseptal puncture (tip at LUPV), or RVOT/PA views, or 3D ICE. ICE was slightly less sensitive than TOE for LAA in a pre-cardioversion study (E20 p.27).

**TAVI** (E20 p.30-31): TOE probe sits behind AV and blocks fluoroscopic valve plane and must be pulled back during aortography; ICE gives LVOT (Fig 6) and AV short-axis (Fig 11) images from a stable RA position. Evidence small.

**EP** (E20 p.2, 13): main imaging for transseptal access and PVI (left PV view), flutter ablation (RAA/crista view); fewer complications reported.

**Complications / pericardial effusion** (E20 p.30): rapid detection of thrombus on wires/devices, perforation, effusion. Effusion: RV position, angle right, inferior LV wall. ICE-specific risks: perforation, atrial arrhythmia.

## 4. ICE vs TOE

Advantages (E20 p.1-2)
- No sedation/GA, no oesophageal intubation; more comfortable.
- Some structures better seen than by TOE (e.g. aortic arch branches from SVC).
- Less fluoroscopy time; interventionist can run it alone (TOE needs an echocardiographer); fewer staff.
- TAVI: no probe shadow over the valve plane.

Limitations
- Extra venous access; catheter perforation/arrhythmia risk (E20 p.22, 30).
- High unit cost of single-use catheters limits uptake; resterilisation only in some countries (E20 p.5).
- 2D ICE sector is a single side-looking plane; far-field (LAA) resolution suboptimal from RA (E20 p.30).
- 3D TOE better for en-face mitral views and full catheter tip visualisation (E20 p.27).
- Learning curve: not stated numerically; chapter says mastering basics is worthwhile.

## 5. Numbers

- Mechanical: 9 Fr, 110 cm, 9 MHz, 1 cm housing, 360° image; Convoy sheaths 15-140°, start with 55°.
- Phased array: 5-10 MHz; 64 crystals; sector 90°; AcuNav 8 Fr/110 cm, 10 Fr/90 cm; deflection 160° (AcuNav), 120° (ViewFlex); ViewFlex 9 Fr.
- 3D/4D: 90°x24° (AcuNav V, 10 Fr), 90°x50° (Volume, 12.5 Fr, 90 cm), 90°x90° (NuVera, 10 Fr); MicroLinear 14 MHz; HockeyStick 7-12 MHz; cMUT 5 Fr lumen.
- View angles (CW from neutral): 15-30, 30-40, 45, 60-70, 70-80, 90-100, 100-150, 150-180, 210-240°; 15° steps.
- Figure depth settings 50-130 mm (most 80-110 mm); 7.5-10 MHz.
- PFO prevalence 20%; ASD rim ≥5 mm in 6 sectors; fluoro-only closure 88-91%; residual 6-7 / 2-3 / 1-2%.
- VSD trial 3/32 (8.6%).
- Misfiled tables (chapter 2.7): AS mild AVA >1.5 cm², MG <25, Vmax <3.0; moderate 1-1.5, 25-40, 3.0-4.0; severe <1.0, >40, >4.0; critical <0.6, >60, >5.0. MS mild >1.5 cm², MG <5, PHT <100 ms; moderate 1-1.5, 5-10, 100-300; severe ≤1.0, >10, >300.
