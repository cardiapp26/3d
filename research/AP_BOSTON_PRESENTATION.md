# Boston accessory localization presentation

User source: `boston accessory local.pptx`, eleven slides. No explicit author, date, or publisher citation found in slide text. Filename does not establish Boston Scientific authorship. Inspected all eleven embedded slide images and native text; originals remain outside application assets.

Slides 1–5/11: annular anatomy and four-step ECG localization summary. Kept as a separate explanatory table because it differs from the preexisting Kardiyopedi decision walk (notably branch order and biphasic categories). No silent replacement of that algorithm.

Slides 6–8: left posterolateral/anterolateral surface ECG examples. Slide 8 labels a left free-wall example despite deep V1 S. Added two original synthetic intracardiac AVRT examples to compare CS sequences. These slides contain no EPS channel recordings, and added delays are not extracted measurements. Same TCL 400 ms and local VA 85 ms as other synthetic location examples isolate activation-sequence differences. CS pair numbers are illustrative, not universal position criteria.

Slide 9: coronary sinus diverticulum and middle cardiac vein examples. Negative initial delta in lead II is described as a clue to coronary venous mapping, not an automatic epicardial diagnosis or procedural instruction.

Slide 10: retrograde P-wave polarity. Explicitly separated from delta-wave localization and supplemented with the reported negative lead-I P-wave exception in anteroseptal pathways. P/T overlap and approximate localization stated.

Independent primary-source checks:

- Arruda et al., 1998, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-8167.1998.tb00861.x: initial delta and coronary venous location.
- Tai et al., 1997, DOI 10.1016/S0735-1097(96)00490-1: retrograde P-wave morphology.
- Rostock et al., 2008, PMID 18415672: T-wave-subtracted retrograde P polarity; negative/isoelectric V1 right-sided and positive V1 left-sided in the study.
- PMID 9230171: negative lead-I retrograde P during anteroseptal AP-mediated AVRT, preventing a deterministic left-free-wall rule.
